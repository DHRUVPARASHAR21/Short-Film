# Master Creative Brief

## Title
Life in 20 Seconds

## Format
Silent animated short film.

## Runtime
Approximately 20 seconds.

## Story
A boy sees a pale white butterfly and begins chasing it. Through fluid transitions, he grows from child to teenager, falls in love, builds a home, becomes a father, watches his son leave, loses his wife, grows old, and finally encounters the butterfly again. This time he does not chase it. He lets it land, then releases it. Spring returns and another child runs after the same butterfly while the camera remains still.

## Meaning
The butterfly is not death or happiness. It represents the thing just ahead of us: the next achievement, person, phase, destination, or version of life.

The tragedy is not that the protagonist dies. It is that only near the end does he realize how much of life happened while he was looking toward what came next.

## Emotional Arc
Wonder → pursuit → love → belonging → parenthood → acceleration → separation → loss → acceptance → renewal.

## Visual Philosophy
- mature hand-drawn historical anime
- naturalistic anatomy
- restrained facial acting
- expressive eyes and hands
- painterly countryside backgrounds
- subtle textured linework
- muted earth tones
- cinematic natural lighting
- timeless pre-industrial rural setting
- poetic but grounded
- no dialogue, narration, or explanatory text

## Recurring Motifs
### Butterfly
Represents "whatever comes next."

### Hands
The film repeatedly rhymes hands across life:
- child reaching for butterfly
- lover touching lover
- baby gripping father's finger
- son embracing father
- old hand receiving butterfly
- old hand resting among flowers

## Ending Principle
No quote, no philosophical title card, no explanation. The final image must complete the metaphor by itself.
