# Camera, Transition, Color, and Motion Rules

## Camera Grammar

### Childhood
- camera chases him
- energetic tracking
- low angles
- movement and discovery

### Love
- camera becomes gentler
- medium shots
- slow pushes
- more stillness

### Family
- stable frames
- balanced compositions
- home feels secure

### Son Leaving
- wider framing
- more negative space
- slower holds

### Wife Gone
- camera almost stops moving

### Old Age
- minimal movement
- longer visual holds

### Final Child
The child runs away, but the camera does not follow.

This is a formal statement of the film's thesis.

## Transition System
Use visual rhymes, not random cuts.

Primary hand motif:
1. child reaches for butterfly
2. lover touches lover
3. baby grips father's finger
4. son embraces father
5. old hand receives butterfly
6. old hand rests among flowers

Secondary transition device:
- characters pass behind objects and emerge older
- identical compositions transform across seasons
- repeated hand positions create time jumps

## Seasonal Color Script

### 0–5 sec
Spring: fresh green, pale blue, gold.

### 5–10 sec
Late summer: amber, warm red, honey light.

### 10–13 sec
Golden autumn: ochre, brown, subdued green.

### 13–15 sec
Late autumn: cooler browns and greys.

### 15–19.4 sec
Winter: blue-grey, white, desaturated brown.

### 19.4–20 sec
Spring again: fresh green, white flowers, sky blue.

The ending palette should echo the opening.

## Movement Hierarchy

### High Motion
- child running
- leap
- house-building flashes
- father lifting son
- son leaving

### Medium Motion
- walking
- holding hands
- embracing
- reaching

### Low Motion
- eyes
- hands
- grass
- hair
- butterfly
- empty room
- winter
- final shot

## Acting Rules
No anime melodrama.

Emotion is communicated through:
- eyes
- posture
- distance
- hands
- hesitation
- small smiles
- objects left behind

No screaming, sobbing, theatrical collapse, or exaggerated crying.
