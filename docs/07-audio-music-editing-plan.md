# Audio, Music, and Editing Plan

## Dialogue / Narration
None.

The film must remain understandable without spoken language or explanatory text.

## Sound Design
Generate video without model audio. Build sound in post for consistency.

Recurring sound palette:
- wind through grass
- footsteps
- cloth movement
- distant birds
- wood creaks
- fireplace
- baby's breathing
- room tone
- winter wind
- extremely subtle butterfly flutter

The same wind motif should connect all life stages.

## Music
Minimal and intimate.

Possible instrumentation:
- soft piano
- solo cello
- restrained bowed strings
- light folk texture

Avoid:
- giant cinematic swells
- trailer percussion
- manipulative sentimentality
- triumphant finale

### Musical Arc

#### 0–5 sec
Simple, curious motif.

#### 5–10 sec
Harmony opens and warms.

#### 10–15 sec
Tempo or internal pulse subtly accelerates.

#### 15–18 sec
Instrumentation falls away.

#### 18–19.4 sec
Near silence around the butterfly landing.

#### 19.4–20 sec
Opening motif returns in a peaceful but not fully resolved form.

## Sonic Emphasis at the End
At the old man's butterfly moment, almost everything should disappear.

Prefer:
- wind
- breath
- faint flutter

Then spring returns.

## Editing Philosophy
The audience experiences approximately 80 years in 20 seconds.

Life should not progress at a uniform rate.

Early childhood:
- rapid, playful visual beats

Love / family:
- slightly longer emotional holds

Adulthood:
- faster montage compression

Goodbye:
- rhythm slows

Loss:
- visual silence

Acceptance:
- longest subjective pause

## Emotional Anchor Shots
These should receive more perceived time than surrounding beats:
- first eye contact
- baby's hand gripping father's finger
- son's embrace
- empty side of bed
- butterfly landing on old hand
- final child running away

## Final Shot
The child runs toward the distance.

The camera remains still.

No quote.
No title explanation.
No voiceover.

Cut to black.
